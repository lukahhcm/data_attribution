\begin{abstract}
Data attribution assigns a score to each training example, whereas data
selection must repeatedly decide which examples to train on under a finite
budget. We connect these tasks through a common bilevel program over
continuous example weights. In this formulation, the value-function penalty
used by fully first-order stochastic bilevel optimization (F2SA) yields a
two-model, per-example loss difference. The idealized form of Reducible
Holdout Loss Selection (RHO-LOSS) has exactly the same direction at a fixed
weight vector, while practical RHO-LOSS introduces an additional frozen
holdout-model approximation. This distinction exposes why a one-shot
attribution score need not solve data selection and identifies the algorithmic
choices that can create gains in a multi-round procedure. We turn the
connection into three falsifiable research questions: whether the predicted
gradient correspondence holds in finite neural-network training, whether
iterative optimization of persistent weights improves over a single score at
uniform weights, and which approximations determine the
performance--computation trade-off. Our experimental protocol separates
inner-solution accuracy, outer update frequency, batch size, budget, and
continuous-to-discrete projection, with correctness gates preceding all
large-scale comparisons.
\end{abstract}
