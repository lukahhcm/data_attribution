\begin{abstract}
Popular training-data attribution methods appear to use different principles.
We show instead that three families have a one-to-one correspondence with
standard approximations to the gradient of one bilevel data-weighting problem,
evaluated at the same outer state: Influence Functions implement implicit
differentiation, trajectory-specific leave-one-out methods implement gradient
unrolling, and idealized RHO-LOSS implements a value-function direction. This
unification also exposes a shared limitation for data selection: each method
produces one local direction, whereas selection requires solving a constrained
outer problem along a changing training trajectory. We study this gap through
the value-function--RHO branch, whose loss-difference form admits both a strict
two-inner implementation and an efficient online approximation. Under a fixed
target-training horizon, we compare one to five complete inner-solve/outer-
update rounds, reset and persistent continuous weights, and the canonical
Influence Function and unrolling attribution baselines. We then factor
practical RHO into global versus batch-local quotas, stale versus online scores,
immediate updates, and frozen versus updating comparators. The design tests
whether gains come from optimizing utility beyond one attribution step, merely
recomputing a local score, or the online protocol rather than its score formula.
\end{abstract}
