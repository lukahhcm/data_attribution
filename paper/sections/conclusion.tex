\section{Conclusion}

Influence Functions, trajectory-specific unrolling, and ideal RHO-LOSS are not
isolated attribution principles: they correspond respectively to implicit,
unrolled, and value-function approximations of a common bilevel outer
direction. This is the paper's first insight. Its consequence is also shared:
each attribution method supplies a local direction, not a solution to
fixed-budget data selection. We investigate that gap through the VF--RHO
branch, where the strict loss difference and practical online approximation can
be separated cleanly. The fixed-horizon experiment compares one-shot
selection, repeated reset scoring, and persistent continuous optimization after
identical real target training; the RHO ladder then separates score fidelity
from local quota, immediate updates, online rescoring, and batch scale.
Together, these tests determine whether multi-round gains come from optimizing
utility beyond one attribution step or from the practical mechanics of online
training.
