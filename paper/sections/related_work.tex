\section{Related Work}

Training-data attribution estimates how individual examples affect a learned
model or a downstream objective. Influence functions approximate the effect
of infinitesimal reweighting through an inverse-Hessian expression
\citep{koh2017influence}; unrolling instead differentiates through a finite
training trajectory. These methods naturally provide local directions, but
budgeted data selection additionally requires a feasible outer state and a
rule for applying multiple decisions.

F2SA is a fully first-order method for stochastic bilevel optimization
\citep{kwon2023f2sa}. It replaces second-order hypergradient calculations by
tracking the minimizers of a lower objective and a penalized upper-plus-lower
objective, while increasing the penalty multiplier and coordinating step
sizes. Its theory assumes an unconstrained outer variable and a strongly
convex lower objective. The present work uses its estimator structure but
does not transfer those guarantees to projected neural-network data
selection.

RHO-LOSS selects examples with high current loss but low irreducible loss
under a model trained on holdout data \citep{mindermann2022rholoss}. It was
designed for online selection from a large pre-batch and emphasizes examples
that are learnable, worth learning, and not yet learned. We distinguish its
ideal joint-model derivation from the frozen IL-model approximation used by
the practical algorithm, and place both in the common bilevel notation of
Section~\ref{sec:connection}.
