\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rqs}

\paragraph{RQ1: When does the RHO loss difference behave as the predicted
value-function descent direction?}
The analytical answer is Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho-vf-equivalence}: ideal RHO and the
negative finite-penalty VF gradient coincide when their objectives,
normalizations, weight vector, and inner solutions coincide. The empirical
answer measures how this correspondence degrades under finite inner training,
a nonconverged target, and the frozen IL approximation. RQ1 is answered by
gradient agreement and ranking stability, not primarily by final test
accuracy.
The preregistered hypothesis is that converged ideal RHO and VF agree up to
numerical error, while most of the practical gap is explained by the frozen
comparator and incomplete inner tracking rather than by the loss-difference
identity itself.

\paragraph{RQ2: Does iterative optimization improve data selection beyond a
single attribution direction at uniform weights?}
We compare a one-shot update from $\vomega^{\mathrm{unif}}$ with multiple
updates of a persistent, budget-feasible continuous state. The target data
budget is held fixed. We vary the number of target epochs between outer
updates and require adequate inner tracking before interpreting a multi-round
gain. RQ2 is answered by paired differences in downstream performance,
selection quality, and compute, together with the trajectory of $\vomega$.
The primary estimand is
$\Delta_{\mathrm{multi}}=A(\mathrm{VF\mbox{-}M})-
A(\mathrm{VF\mbox{-}1})$. We claim a multi-round benefit only when its paired
interval is positive and the same conclusion survives an
accuracy-versus-compute comparison.

\paragraph{RQ3: Which approximations determine the
performance--computation frontier?}
We factor the path from ideal VF to practical RHO into the comparator
(joint/updating versus holdout-only/frozen), inner accuracy, candidate scope
(global versus batch-local), outer update interval, and continuous-to-discrete
selection. RQ3 asks which components explain the gap between the theoretically
motivated direction and the best practical selector. It is answered by a
nested ablation ladder rather than by an unstructured method sweep.
The hypothesis is not that the most faithful estimator always wins. Rather,
the frozen IL and batch-local approximations may improve the practical
frontier even while reducing agreement with the ideal direction. This RQ
separates estimator fidelity from downstream usefulness.

Together, the RQs support the paper's central claim only if the following
logic holds: RQ1 validates the local connection; RQ2 tests whether optimizing
that direction repeatedly creates a positive multi-round benefit; and RQ3
locates the source and cost of that benefit. A failure of RQ1 blocks a
theoretical interpretation of RQ2, although the resulting heuristic may still
be reported as such.
